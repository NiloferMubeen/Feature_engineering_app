Date-Time Derived Features

Timestamps hold a goldmine of signals — day of week, hour, seasonality, holidays, and time-since events. Learn to extract every bit of temporal signal.

The Concept

Unlocking the Power of Time

A raw timestamp like 2024-12-25 22:15:00 is nearly useless to a model. But from it, we can extract: it's Christmas (holiday), it's a Wednesday, it's 10 PM (late night), it's Q4, it's winter — all incredibly powerful signals!

Models can't inherently understand that Friday evenings drive more restaurant orders, or that Q4 boosts retail. You have to encode this temporal knowledge as features.

Real-World Examples

Temporal Features in Action

🚕 Ride Hailing
Surge Pricing Prediction
Uber/Ola models use hour-of-day, day-of-week, is_weekend, and is_holiday features extracted from timestamps to predict demand surges and set dynamic prices.
🏦 Banking
Fraud Detection
Fraud models use is_night_transaction, days_since_last_transaction, and hour_of_day — a transaction at 3 AM in a foreign country is suspicious; a timestamp alone won't tell you that.

🔑 Key Terms
Timestamp
Timestamp
A specific point in time, typically stored as datetime or Unix epoch seconds.
Cyclical Enc.
Cyclical Encoding
Encoding cyclic features (hour, month) as sin/cos pairs so hour 23 is close to hour 0.
Seasonality
Seasonality
Regular patterns that repeat over time — daily, weekly, or yearly cycles in data.
Lag Feature
Lag Feature
The value of a feature N time steps in the past — crucial for time-series forecasting.
🔄 Cyclical Encoding Tip

Hours 0 and 23 are adjacent in time but far apart numerically. Use sin/cos encoding:

hour_sin = sin(2π × hour / 24)
hour_cos = cos(2π × hour / 24)
