ANOVA F-Test

Analysis of Variance compares means across multiple groups to determine if a continuous feature varies significantly with a categorical target.

The Concept

Variance Between Groups

ANOVA asks: does the mean value of this feature differ significantly across target classes? If yes, the feature is informative. The F-statistic is the ratio of between-group variance to within-group variance.

High F-score = group means are far apart relative to spread within groups = feature discriminates between classes well.

F = Between-Group Variance / Within-Group Variance
= (SS_between / df_between) / (SS_within / df_within)
Real-World Examples

ANOVA in Action

🌸 Iris Dataset
Petal Length vs Species
Setosa has very short petals (mean 1.5cm), while Virginica has long petals (mean 5.6cm). ANOVA F-score for petal_length is ~1180 — one of the most discriminative features possible!
💰 Salary Prediction
Experience vs Job Level
Years of experience differs significantly across Junior/Mid/Senior/Lead levels. ANOVA reveals this, confirming that years_of_experience is a high-value feature for predicting job level.

🔑 Key Terms
F-Statistic
F-Statistic
Ratio of between-group to within-group variance. High F = groups differ a lot = feature is useful.
SS_between
Between-Group SS
Sum of squared deviations of group means from the overall mean — captures class separation.
SS_within
Within-Group SS
Sum of squared deviations within each group — captures internal noise/spread.
f_classif
f_classif (sklearn)
Computes ANOVA F-value for each feature vs a classification target.
📊 ANOVA vs Correlation

Use Correlation when both feature and target are continuous.

Use ANOVA when the feature is continuous but the target is categorical (classification tasks).
