Interaction Features

Capture the combined effect of two features by multiplying, dividing, or creating ratios — revealing patterns no single feature can show alone.

The Concept

When Two Features Work Together

Interaction features capture the joint effect of two or more variables. Linear models can only model additive effects — but by multiplying features, you let them model multiplicative relationships too.

Example: Price alone doesn't predict demand. Discount alone doesn't either. But Price × Discount captures the real interaction — a high discount on an expensive item drives far more sales.

Real-World Examples

Interaction Features in Action

🏠 Real Estate
Area × Floors Interaction
Property value depends on both floor area AND number of floors. Their product captures a "building mass" concept that helps predict prices better than either alone.
area_x_floors = area_sqft × num_floors
🛒 E-Commerce
CTR × Price Interaction
A product with a high click-through rate but very low price suggests impulse buys. CTR × Price captures a "revenue intent" signal for recommendation models.
revenue_intent = ctr × price

🔑 Key Terms
Interaction
Interaction Term
A feature formed by combining two features — usually by multiplication — to capture their joint effect.
Multiplicative
Multiplicative Effect
When the effect of one variable depends on the level of another — classic non-linearity.
Ratio Feature
Ratio Feature
A / B — normalizes one feature by another, e.g. sales per employee.
PolynomialFeatures
sklearn PolynomialFeatures
Auto-generates all pairwise interactions with degree=2 and interaction_only=True.
